What is Table Talk?

The Mission Mississippi Table Talk Cohort Initiative brings together 4 core members — with spouses welcome, up to 8 total — for monthly shared meals and guided conversation, fostering Christ-centered dialogue on race, reconciliation, and community oneness.

Each cohort shares 8 meals across the year using Table Talk cards, then joins all cohorts for a 9th celebratory gathering — building relationships on truth, grace, understanding, and trust.

What to Expect  hover to expand
Expect: Difficulty
Conversations around racial healing are generally difficult. Our shared history includes pain, distrust, failures, and resentment. We must fully expect that sometimes it will be challenging to fully unpack.

Expect: Discomfort
Challenging conversations can be uncomfortable, but so is growth. In the discomfort, remind yourself of the benefits of growing in your understanding and regard for those around you.

Expect: Disagreements
Because our experiences, backgrounds, and social circles differ, we should not be surprised when we disagree. Prepare for it and think about healthy ways to engage it.

Tips for a Successful Year
Schedule All Eight Meals Upfront
Put every date on the calendar before anyone leaves the first gathering. Groups that lock in the full year have far better completion rates than those who plan month to month.
Spread Breaks Intentionally
Three break windows across the year. Consider June, July, and January. Decide early and hold to it.
Covenant to Stay at the Table
Commit together — verbally, as a group — to remain for the full year. These conversations have ebbs and flows. That commitment holds the group through the harder sessions.
Know What You're Working Toward
Every participant who completes the full cohort year receives a certificate of completion at the year-end gathering. Finish well.
Read the Ground Rules at Every Talk
Make it a standing ritual — someone reads them aloud from the app at the start of each gathering. Do not paraphrase them. The exact wording matters.
Rotate Who Hosts
Shared ownership deepens the cohort and prevents uneven burden on one relationship.
Protect the Meal
The food is part of the ministry. Linger over it before the cards come out.
Name a Point of Contact
One person who nudges the group when momentum stalls. Not a leader — just a coordinator who sends the reminder and confirms the location.
Give Yourself Permission to Linger
The most significant moments often happen after the last card. Don't rush out.
Pray Together
Open and close each gathering in prayer. This is gospel-rooted work — let it be gospel-grounded in practice.
At the Year-End Gathering
Come ready to celebrate, share what you learned, and recruit. Every participant is encouraged to invite three new individuals to form a new cohort for the coming year. The movement grows one table at a time.
